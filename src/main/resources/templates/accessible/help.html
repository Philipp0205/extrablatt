What this is

Websites publish a plain list of everything they have just written. It is called a feed. This app collects the feeds you follow and puts all their articles in one place, newest first, with no adverts and no clutter.

You do not need to know anything about feeds to use it. You choose subjects; we look after the addresses.

Following a topic

Open Add a topic and press the button under any subject that interests you — blindness and low vision, clinical trials, eye research, health, world news, and others.

One press follows several publications at once. Their articles then appear together under that topic's name on your Topics page. If one publication in a topic cannot be reached, the rest are still added and we tell you which one was skipped.

Following one website

Go to your sources and type the plain address of the site, like bbc.com. That is all. You do not have to hunt for an "RSS" link, and you do not have to type https:// or anything ending in .xml. We look at the site and find its feed for you.

If we cannot find one, the site does not publish a feed. That happens, and it is not something you did wrong.

Reading an article

Press an article's title to open it. The article is fetched and stripped down to its text: no menus, no pop-ups, no adverts. Pictures are hidden unless you ask for them.

By default, an article opens with the main points first: its own headings and bullet points, listed one after another, so you can see what it says before deciding to read all of it. Nothing there is rewritten — every line is taken word for word from the article, which is below it in full. You can switch this off under Display.

Having it read to you

Every article has a Listen to this article button. It uses the voices built into your phone or computer, so it works without an account anywhere else and costs nothing. The part being read is highlighted as it goes. Press the same button again to pause, and Stop to finish.

Most devices have several voices, and some of them sound far better than others. We pick the most natural one we can find and put the rest in the Voice menu next to the button, best first. Change it and you hear a sample straight away; your choice, and the speed, are remembered on this device.

If every voice sounds flat and mechanical, your device has only its oldest ones installed. On Windows, adding a voice under Settings, Time & language, Speech — or opening this page in Microsoft Edge, which brings its own natural voices — makes the biggest difference. On an iPhone or a Mac, download a Premium or Enhanced voice under Spoken Content. On Android, update Speech Recognition & Synthesis from Google.

If you do not see the button, your browser has no voice available. A screen reader will still read the page in its usual way.

Saving something for later

Every article has a Save button, both in the list and while you are reading it. Saved articles are gathered on the Saved page and stay there until you remove them, whether or not you have read them.

Changing how it looks

The A− and A+ buttons at the top of every page make the text smaller and bigger straight away.

The Display page has more: four colour schemes (including black with bright colours, and black with yellow text), five text sizes, three line spacings, wider spacing between letters, and a choice of plain or book letters. Your choices are saved to your account, so they follow you to another device.

To print an article the way you see it, press Print this article at the bottom of it. The colours and the large text are kept.

Keyboard

  • Tab moves to the next link or button, Shift and Tab back.
  • The first thing on every page is a Skip to the main content link.
  • Every page has one main heading, and sections under it, so a screen reader can jump by heading.

If something goes wrong

Nothing here can break your account. If a page looks wrong, the Display page can put the colours and size back.

If you follow newsletters by e-mail, your personal address for them is on the your account page. Subscribe with that address and the issues arrive here as articles.

This app is free and run by one person. If it is useful to you, that is already enough.
